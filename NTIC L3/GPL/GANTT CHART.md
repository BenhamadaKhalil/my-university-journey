# 🎓 **CHAPTER 5 — GANTT CHART **

---

## 🕰 **History of the Gantt Chart**

The **Gantt Chart** was developed by **Henry L. Gantt**, an engineer and management consultant, in the **early 20th century**.

- 🏭 Initially used for **industrial project organization**
    
- ⚔️ Widely applied during **World War I**
    
- 📊 Became a **standard project management tool**
    
- 💻 Now integrated into most **project management software**
    

📌 Its success comes from its **simplicity and visual clarity**.

---

## 🎯 **Main Objective of the Gantt Chart**

The Gantt chart is used to **visualize the project schedule over time**.

### 📐 Axes

- **Horizontal axis (X)** ⏱ → Time (days, weeks, months)
    
- **Vertical axis (Y)** 📋 → Tasks or project phases
    

### 📊 Task Bars

- Each task is shown as a **horizontal bar**
    
- Bar length = **task duration**
    
- Bar position = **start and end dates**
    

📌 Gantt answers the question:  
👉 **WHEN** is each task done?

---

## 🧩 **Main Components of a Gantt Chart**

### 🛠 1️⃣ Tasks

- Activities or phases of the project
    
- Displayed as horizontal bars
    
- Arranged chronologically
    

---

### ⏱ 2️⃣ Durations

- Time allocated to each task
    
- Expressed in days, weeks, or months
    
- Shown by the length of the bar
    

---

### 🔷 3️⃣ Milestones

- Important events or checkpoints
    
- Represented by special symbols (often ◆)
    
- Mark the completion of major phases
    

📌 Milestones have **zero duration**.

---

## 🛠 **How to Build a Gantt Chart**

### Step 1️⃣ Set the Time Scale

- Choose the unit: days, weeks, or months
    

### Step 2️⃣ Place the Tasks

- Draw task bars according to:
    
    - Start date
        
    - Duration
        
    - Dependencies (from planning)
        

📌 Gantt charts are usually built **after WBS and PERT**.

---

## 🔍 **Gantt Chart vs PERT Network**

### 🆚 Comparison

| Aspect        | Gantt Chart           | PERT Network         |
| ------------- | --------------------- | -------------------- |
| View          | Linear (timeline)     | Network (logic)      |
| Focus         | Scheduling & tracking | Dependencies & logic |
| Readability   | Very easy             | More complex         |
| Critical path | Not explicit          | Clearly visible      |
| Best use      | Monitoring & control  | Planning & analysis  |

📌 **Key idea:**  
👉 PERT = logic  
👉 Gantt = calendar

---

## 📊 **Level of Detail**

- **Gantt**
    
    - Macro + micro view
        
    - Good for detailed tracking
        
    - Easy date adjustments
        
- **PERT**
    
    - Mainly macro
        
    - Better for structuring the project
        
    - Less practical for daily updates
        

📌 They are **complementary**, not competing tools.

---

## ⚡ **Advantages of the Gantt Chart (Real-Time Tracking)**

### ⏱ 1️⃣ Time Visibility

- Clear start and end dates
    
- Easy identification of deadlines
    
- Visible milestones
    

---

### 🔄 2️⃣ Task Updates

- Progress can be updated visually
    
- Delays are immediately noticeable
    
- Corrective actions can be taken quickly
    

---

### 🔮 3️⃣ Forecast Adjustment

- Dates can be revised directly
    
- Supports **dynamic planning**
    
- Helps adapt resources and efforts
    

---

### 🗣 4️⃣ Improved Communication

- Easy to understand by all stakeholders
    
- Excellent **sharing and reporting tool**
    
- Aligns team on priorities and deadlines
    

---

## 🔗 **Gantt Chart and Float (Slack)**

- **Total Float** → delay allowed without affecting project end
    
- **Free Float** → delay allowed without affecting successor tasks
    

📌 Tasks with **zero float** are usually **critical tasks**.

---

## 🧠 **PERT + GANTT = Powerful Combination**

Best practice in project management:

- 🕸 **PERT** → build logic & identify critical path
    
- 📅 **GANTT** → visualize schedule & track progress
    

📌 This combination ensures:

- Better planning
    
- Better monitoring
    
- Better control of deadlines and resources
