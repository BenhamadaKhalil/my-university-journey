## 📌 What is an Interactive System?

A system that allows **two-way communication** between the user and the computer.

The loop looks like this:

```
User acts → Computer responds → User reacts → ...
```

> 💡 **Key idea:** The computer doesn't just "do its thing" — it _reacts_ to what the user does (clicks, typing, touch...).

**Examples:** Smartphones 📱 · Video games 🎮 · Websites 🌐 · Word processors 📝 · Mobile apps

---

## 📊 Levels of Interaction

|Level|Type|Example|How it works|
|---|---|---|---|
|1️⃣|**No interaction**|Compiler|You give input → it processes silently → gives output. No dialogue.|
|2️⃣|**Limited interaction**|Traffic lights 🚦|Follows a fixed timer automatically. Users just respond to it.|
|3️⃣|**Fully interactive**|Games, apps 🎮|Every click/tap produces an immediate response. User is in full control.|

## 🧩 Key Terminology — Be Careful!

> ⚠️ **HCI** is one acronym with **two meanings** (a classic exam trap!):

|Acronym|Meaning (EN)|Meaning (FR)|What it covers|
|---|---|---|---|
|**HCI**|Human-Computer **Interaction**|IHM : Interaction(s) Homme-Machine|The full _process_ — behaviors, workflows, feedback loops, UX|
|**HCI**|Human-Computer **Interface**|IHM : Interface(s) Homme-Machine|The physical/digital _components_ — keyboards, screens, buttons|

> 🔬 **In this course, we focus on Human-Computer _Interaction_ (the broader concept).**

### 🧠 A deeper look

At first glance: _human interacts with computer → computer responds._

But in reality: **the human interacts with a task _through_ the computer.** The computer is just the mediator.

> ✍️ When you're writing an essay, are you thinking about the keyboard? **No** — you're thinking about your words. The tool disappears when it's good.

**Good tool** → You're so focused on the task you forget the tool exists (e.g. a smooth app, a good pen). **Bad tool** → You spend more time fighting the tool than doing the task (e.g. confusing software, a complicated remote).

## 📅 Historical Timeline of HCI

```
1940s        → Computers appear: huge, expensive, locked in special rooms.
                Only trained engineers used them. Commands like "grep".
                Nobody cared about ease of use.

1940–1980    → Computers exist but aren't user-friendly.
                Specialists only. Normal people = zero access.

1980s 🚀     → Everything changes. Computers move into offices & homes.
                HCI emerges to make them accessible to everyone.

1983 ⭐      → BIRTH OF HCI — Three major events:
                  • First ACM SIGCHI conference (people using computers)
                  • Important book on computer psychology published
                  • Apple announces the Macintosh 🍎 (designed for everyone)
```

## 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Why HCI is Challenging — Human Variability

### Computers are simple. Humans are complex.

- Computers: built to follow specific instructions, work the **same way every time**.
- Humans: **varied, unpredictable, ever-changing** — making design much harder.

### 🌍 Ways humans differ:

|Dimension|Example|
|---|---|
|👶👴 **Age**|A 7-year-old uses tech very differently from a 70-year-old|
|♿ **Physical abilities**|Some are colorblind, visually impaired, or have limited hand movement|
|🧑‍💻 **Experience**|A tech expert navigates easily; a beginner gets lost on the same screen|
|🌍 **Language & culture**|Reading direction: left-to-right vs. right-to-left (Arabic, Hebrew)|
|😴 **Temporary state**|Tired or stressed users behave differently than alert ones|

### 💥 Real-world impact examples:

- 🖱️ A button easy to click with a mouse → **impossible** for someone with trembling hands
- 🔤 Normal-size text → **too small** for someone with poor vision
- 🇬🇧 An English-only app → **useless** for Arabic-only speakers
- ⚡ A reflex-based game → **fun** for young players, **frustrating** for seniors

## ❓ The Big Questions HCI Asks

> Small, specific questions = answerable. Big questions about human behavior = much harder.

- 🤔 Why do people **make mistakes** when using technology?
- 🧠 Why do people **forget** features they've used before?
- 😤 Why is **installing software** so frustrating?
- 📱 Why is **driving while on the phone** so dangerous?
- 📲 Why are people so **drawn to social media** (Facebook, Twitter...)?

## 🔑 Core Principle: "Know the User"

> You **cannot** design one system that works perfectly for _everyone_ — that's impossible.

Instead, successful HCI designers:

1. ✅ **Identify** their target users
2. ✅ **Study** how those specific people behave and what they need
3. ✅ **Test** designs with real users from different backgrounds
4. ✅ **Adjust** based on feedback

> 💬 _"The more we understand humans, the better our chances of designing systems that work as intended. Our work is never precise — it is always **approximate**."_
